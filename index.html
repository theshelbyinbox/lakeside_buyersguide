Fall 2019 — How It Started

It all started with my mom on a swather.

She'd been out cutting hay all day. And if you've never done the farming thing.. the thoughts going through your head while you're driving circles around a field for hours are kind of like shower thoughts... except on steroids.

Who knows what the heck kind of crazy ideas you might dream up while you're out there!

That day, her crazy swather idea landed in my text messages. She asked, “Do you think you'd ever want to move back to Christmas Valley if you had your own coffee shop to run out here or something?”

We went from crazy swather idea, to hearing the Lakeside was up for sale, to okay wait.. are we being serious about this?? To signing papers — all in about 1.5 months!

We got the keys January 1st, 2020.

well that escalated quickly ....

January 2020 — The Honest Before

Nobody would ever call it easy.

My dad walked through the Lakeside before we bought it, and while yes — there were aesthetic issues all around — the problems went a lot deeper than that.

(Which, in typical fashion, we only REALLY understood after we'd started ripping the sheetrock off the walls. No turning back at that point!)

The whole property was running on infrastructure from the 1960s. The year it was BUILT.

Our first winter, the restaurant roof had at least 5 leaks going at the same time whenever it rained or the snow melted.

We put out buckets. Many of them.

But let me be clear about something —

It's not that the previous owners had failed it. Opposite, actually. They ran the Lakeside (then called the Lakeside Terrace) faithfully and successfully for 25 years!

Their way was simple: keep it old-fashioned, skip the renovations, and just run their little mom-and-pop. It worked for a quarter of a century — and we mean that with real admiration.

If we're being honest, that's the whole difference between their 25 years and our project-heavy 7: they ran it. We rebuilt it. (More on what that means for you — and the books — in a minute.)

The Lakeside didn't need rescuing. It needed updating. That's the part we came to do — and DO we did.

2020–2022 — The Family Rebuild

We gutted the restaurant on day one.

Literally day one. Walked in with the keys and started pulling sheetrock off the walls.

New insulation. All new flooring. Remodeled top to bottom in about a month and a half.

My grandpa hand-painted trim for hours. My sister-in-law was the design mastermind — she picked out the chandeliers. Those kinda-half-painted wood paneled walls and torn-to-shreds carpet turned into a beautifully designed, modern masterpiece! (if you ask me)

The whole room finally made sense. People who knew the old place (every person in Christmas Valley) would walk in and just say, “Whoa.”

That was the winter of 2020.

We spent the next two winters doing the same thing to the motel rooms — every room gutted to the studs and rebuilt from the floor up.

One of those springs in between, my mom repainted the entire exterior restaurant building herself. The next fall we painted the exterior of each motel building. The entire roof on both the restaurant and motel — replaced. A brand new lawn went in somewhere in between all of that.

Eventually we got to the bathhouse remodel. It needed it SOOO bad. When we opened it up we found more rotten boards than good ones, hidden mold, and zero ventilation — so of course we added fans and ventilation to keep that problem from ever happening again.

We replaced the septic system for the restaurant, then the motel septic a couple years later. And when the very old steel drain field in the RV Park corroded in 2024? That was rebuilt too. For a very pretty penny. But we did it right anyway.

That's the kind of place you're buying. The receipts are in a binder.

2020–2023 — The Restaurant Years

For three years, this was the place in town.

We didn't just rebuild the restaurant — we ran it.

Breakfast plates before work. Burgers and fries at lunch. Cinnamon rolls next to a Lakeside mug. Milkshakes shared in a booth.

It was the only diner in town, it was busy, and the crew who ran it with us became family.

We ran it successfully for three years — right up until I burnt out. (Rebuilding a motel and running a restaurant at the same time will do that.)

That's the honest version. And it's also the whole point of “What It Could Be” further down: the kitchen, the equipment, and the demand are all still here — waiting for someone whose energy is fresh.

Mostly, though? People just loved it here. They still do.

The Operating System

It came with instructions that could fit on an index card. It leaves with an owners manual.

Here's the secret nobody selling a small motel wants to say out loud: most of them run on somebody's memory.

When we took over in 2020, everything I was told about this place fit on the back of a few index cards and an envelope. That was the whole operating system.

But that's just not how I roll. THIS type of stuff is actually my most favorite passion in life — truly! Not joking. I excel at taking the messy, disorganized way a business runs and building out the resources, systems and documentation that make things feel ... easier. Less stressful. More clear.

With the Lakeside, I took essentially nothing as far as documentation goes, and built a full operating system that anyone who can read would be able to figure out — a 116-page owners manual covering every aspect of the business.

Detailed cleaning checklists for housekeeping. Guest forms and employee onboarding materials. How to winterize the property — what to do every spring, summer, fall, and every month in between. Guest reservation and check-in email systems. Every vendor and contact on one sheet.

Even an AI help desk trained on the Lakeside's operations. Type in a question about how anything works here — it answers.

None of that is theoretical. The current on-site manager uses the manual every single day.

And you get to choose how you run it. We ran the whole place fully virtually for two years — guests booked online, got a text with their lockbox code and WiFi password, and let themselves in. Just like an Airbnb!

We never got one 3 a.m. “I locked myself out of my room” text — they have the code to the spare in the lockbox outside their door. Our virtual check-in process is actually one of our highest-rated categories on the guest feedback forms too.

Read This Before You Read the P&L

The books show a renovation, not a business.

Here's the conversation most sellers hope you won't start — so we'll start it ourselves.

When you request the financials (you should — they're available to qualified buyers), the recent profit line is not going to wow you. We know. There's a reason, and it's sitting in every photo on this page.

Remember the couple who ran this place for 25 years? Their model worked because they just ran it. No projects, no renovations, no updates — a steady, old-fashioned mom-and-pop, profitable the whole way through. That's 25 years of proof that this business works when someone simply operates it.

We did the opposite — on purpose. For seven years, nearly every month, money that could have been profit went back into the property instead. Gutted rooms. New exterior. Rebuilt bathhouse. New drain field. Remodeled restaurant. Systems, signage, all of it.

Our books don't show a business that can't make money. They show a business that funded its own top-to-bottom rebuild — while staying open, staying spotless, and holding a 94% guest rating the entire time.

So here's what you're actually walking into: the reinvestment era is over. Roughly 90% of what this property needed, we already did. You'd be the first owner in decades who gets to just run the Lakeside — modern property, proven demand, documented systems — without pouring the profits into catching up.

They proved the business for 25 years. We rebuilt the property for 7. You're the chapter where those two finally happen at the same time.

The High Desert, Three Hours from Anywhere

The scenery photographs. The feeling doesn't.

Photos can show you the lake. They can't play you the sound of it — the geese and ducks guests wake up to, the pelicans that arrive every year like they booked in advance.

Christmas Valley is remote high desert and a genuine dark-sky area. Almost no light pollution. The kind of stars people drive hours for — here, they're just what happens at night.

You're minutes from Fort Rock and the cave where the Northern Paiute lived for thousands of years. Near the Summer Lake Wildlife Area. Down the road from the Cowboy Dinner Tree, which sends us one-nighters all season. Sand dunes for off-roading. Crack in the Ground. The Lost Forest.

Whether you're watching the sunset over the water or standing in the stillness after dark — there's something out here you have to stand in to understand.

From Our Family to Yours

We finished what we came to do.

In 2020, our family bought a tired property that everyone in town had circled and nobody had jumped on.

We spent the years since restoring it building by building — and turning how-to-run-it knowledge that fit on a few index cards into a complete, documented operating system. The Lakeside today is clean, systematized, loved by its guests, and able to run staffed or virtually.

That was the work. And it's done.

Our family's season has changed — my parents are large-scale farmers with an operation that consumes them from spring dirt work through haying, and I've built a business of my own where my heart and energy live now.

The truth about us is that we're builders: we pour everything into a place, leave it better than we found it, and know when it's time to hand it to the people meant to run it. The Lakeside deserves owners whose next chapter it is.

I won't pretend it was easy. We set out buckets under a leaking roof our first winter. We made hard calls. But this place gave our family seven years of livelihood, taught me everything I know about running a business, and gave our guests — by their own words, over a thousand of them — a clean, peaceful place on the water they keep coming back to.

We're not walking away from something broken. We're handing forward something we finished — to whoever is ready to write the part we weren't meant to.

— Shelby, for the whole family

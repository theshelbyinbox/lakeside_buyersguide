Fall 2019 — How It Started

It started with my mom on a swather.

She'd been out cutting hay too long with nothing but her own thoughts, and she texted me a question: would I ever want to live in Christmas Valley if I had my own business to run? A week later, sitting at the post office, she mentioned the Lakeside was for sale — and had been for a while.

Everybody in town had circled it. Nobody had jumped.

We got the keys January 1st, 2020.

January 2020 — The Honest Before

Nobody would ever call it easy.

My dad didn't sugarcoat it: there were definite issues, and lots of things that needed fixed. Peaked ceiling beams stained an ugly red. Tired carpet everywhere. Motel rooms that were old and musty, with window box AC units and infrastructure dating back to the 1960s. That first winter, the restaurant roof had more than five leaks going at once.

We set out buckets.

But underneath all of it: the Lakeside sits right on the bank of the lake. Guests wake up to geese and ducks, the pelicans come through every year, and the sunrises off that water are something no other property around can offer. It was the only motel and RV park directly in Christmas Valley — the reason it had survived when everything else cycled through owners or closed.

It wasn't failing. It was waiting for a family to care about it.

2020–2022 — The Family Rebuild

Gutted to the studs. Rebuilt together.

The very first day we owned it, we walked into the restaurant and gutted it. Completely. Sheetrock off, new insulation, new flooring — remodeled top to bottom in about a month and a half. My grandpa hand-painted trim for hours. My sister Emily led the design and picked out the chandeliers. Those ugly red beams went almost-black, and the whole room finally made sense. People who knew the old place would walk in and just say, “Whoa.”

Then we spent two years doing the same to the motel — every room gutted down to the studs and rebuilt from the floor up. In spring 2020 my parents repainted the entire exterior themselves, scraping loose paint by hand, borrowing a lift for the peaks. My mom texted me from the ladder:

“It's almost hot out here painting and we are loving it.”

The hardest work was the invisible kind. When we opened up the RV bathhouse we found rotted boards, hidden mold, and no ventilation — so we fixed what nobody would ever see, and added fans so it would stay fixed. When the drain field's old steel corroded in 2024, rebuilding it consumed most of that year's cash. We did it right anyway. That's the kind of place you're buying.

The Operating System

It came with index cards. It leaves with a manual.

When I took over in 2020, everything I was told about running this place fit on the back of a few index cards and an envelope. I turned that into a complete operating system: a 116-page manual covering the whole property, screen-recorded step-by-step walkthroughs, housekeeping checklists with room timers, a freeze protocol for hard winters, templated guest check-in texts, every vendor contact on one sheet — and an AI help desk trained on the Lakeside's operations. Type in a question about how anything works here, and it answers.

None of that is theoretical. The Lakeside runs on those systems today — the current on-site manager uses the manual every single day.

And the property is genuinely flexible. We ran it fully virtually for two years: guests booked online, got a check-in text with their lockbox code and WiFi password, and let themselves in. We never got one 3 a.m. lockout call, and check-in became our single highest-rated category. So a new owner gets a real choice — run it with people at the desk, run it virtually from anywhere, or blend the two. The systems support all three, because I built them on purpose.

The High Desert, Three Hours from Anywhere

The scenery photographs. The feeling doesn't.

Photos can show you the lake, but they can't play you the sound of it — the geese and ducks guests wake up to, the pelicans that come through every year like an event. Christmas Valley is remote high desert and a genuine dark-sky area: almost no light pollution, stars people drive hours for.

You're minutes from Fort Rock and its cave where the Northern Paiute lived for thousands of years, near the Summer Lake Wildlife Area, and down the road from the Cowboy Dinner Tree, which sends us one-nighters all season. Sand dunes for off-roading. Crack in the Ground. The Lost Forest. Whether you're watching the sunset over the water or standing in the stillness after dark, there's something out here you have to stand in to understand.

From Our Family to Yours

We finished what we came to do.

In 2020, our family bought a tired property that everyone in town had circled and nobody had jumped on. We spent the years since restoring it building by building, and turning how-to-run-it knowledge that fit on a few index cards into a complete, documented operating system. The Lakeside today is clean, systematized, loved by its guests, and able to run staffed or virtually.

That was the work. And it's done.

Our family's season has changed — my parents are large-scale farmers with an operation that consumes them from spring dirt work through haying, and I've built a business of my own where my heart and energy live now. The Lakeside deserves owners whose next chapter it is.

I won't pretend it was easy. We set out buckets under a leaking roof our first winter. We made hard calls. But this place gave our family seven years of livelihood, taught me everything I know about running a business, and gave our guests — by their own words, over a thousand of them — a clean, peaceful place on the water they keep coming back to.

We're not walking away from something broken. We're handing forward something we finished — to whoever is ready to write the part we weren't meant to.

— Shelby, for the whole family
